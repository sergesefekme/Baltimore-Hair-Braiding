### Rules
- **The dot is part of the name**, not punctuation. Never write "Mirabelle M" with a space, and never drop the dot.
- **B is always uppercase.** Never "Mirabelle.b".
- In the logo lockup the dot is the only Gilt element — it is the mark of distinction, and it sits full-size on the baseline, never as a superscript.
- Never abbreviate the full name in headings, page titles, or the footer.
- "Mirabelle" alone is correct in body copy and in client testimonials, where it reads as the stylist's name rather than the business.

### The Standard — five dimensions

Every service is measured against the same five things. This is the substance behind the price, and it is Section 05 of the website ("The Standard").

| | Dimension | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Source** | Hair sourced and processed in-house. We know where it came from and what was done to it. |
| **2** | **Technique** | Tension-conscious, scalp-first braiding. The method is the product. |
| **3** | **Scalp Health** | Every service starts and ends with the health of what's under the style. |
| **4** | **Longevity** | Built to hold its shape for weeks, not days. |
| **5** | **Presence** | How you carry it when you walk out. The finished feeling. |

These five doubled as the meaning of "5D" while that was in the name. The name has changed; the standard has not. It no longer needs a label to justify it — it stands as the service checklist your stylists work to, and the site presents it as exactly that.

## 3. Brand Positioning

### What it is

Mirabelle.B is a premium African hair braiding salon in Ashburn that controls the entire chain — sourcing and processing its own hair in-house, then installing it using tension-conscious technique. It takes both booked appointments and walk-ins: **the standard is the same either way.**

That last point is the positioning, not a footnote. Most salons treat walk-ins as the overflow tier — whoever is free, whatever hair is in the drawer. Here the hair and the technique are controlled upstream, so a walk-in gets the same sourced hair and the same tension-conscious install as someone who booked six weeks out. Booking ahead buys you a guaranteed stylist and start time, not a better result.

### The one-sentence positioning statement

> For women who take their hair seriously and are tired of gambling on it, Mirabelle.B is a premium Ashburn braiding salon that sources and processes its own hair in-house — so the quality of what goes on your head is a decision we made, not a bet we placed.

### Where you sit in the market

| Category | What they offer | Where Mirabelle.B differs |
|---|---|---|
| Walk-in braiding shops | Fast, cheap, high volume, unpredictable hair quality | We also take walk-ins — but with controlled hair supply and consistent technique behind them |
| Freelance / home braiders | Personal, affordable, booked by DM | A real space, a real booking system, real accountability |
| Beauty-supply hair + any braider | Hair from a shelf, unknown origin | We source and process the hair ourselves |
| High-end salons without textured specialty | Luxury environment, limited braiding expertise | Luxury environment **and** specialist expertise in protective styling |

### Positioning territory

**The vertically integrated braiding atelier.**

Almost every competitor buys hair from a distributor and hopes. You don't. That's not a marketing angle — it's an operational fact, and it's the single most defensible thing about this business. It should be the loudest thing on the website after the work itself.

### The three proof pillars

Everything on the site must reinforce one of these:

1. **We control the hair.** Sourced and processed in-house. Full traceability from raw hair to finished style.
2. **We protect the scalp.** Tension-conscious technique. Protective styling that actually protects.
3. **We treat this as art.** Technique explained, not hidden. Clients leave understanding what was done and why.

### Price positioning

Premium tier for the Ashburn / Loudoun County market — one of the highest-income counties in the country, which is what supports the tier. Priced above the neighbourhood shops and freelancers, justified by hair quality, technique, punctuality, and environment — not by making people book ahead.

## 4. Target Audience

### Primary audience

**1. The professional (28–45).** Works in healthcare, government, education, law, federal contracting, or corporate Northern Virginia and DC. Books protective styles every 6–8 weeks because she does not have time to do her hair every morning. Pain: braiders who run 3 hours behind, styles too tight to sleep on, hair that sheds or tangles within two weeks. **She will pay more for reliability.** Punctuality is a luxury feature for her.

**2. The loc journey client (25–50).** Committed to a multi-year process and looking for a long-term maintenance home, not a one-off appointment. Pain: inconsistent retwist technique, thinning edges, a stylist who moves away or stops taking clients. **She is the highest lifetime-value client you have** — she returns every 4–6 weeks, indefinitely.

**3. The wig client (30–60).** Two distinct groups, and the site must speak to both without conflating them:
   - *By choice* — versatility, protective styling, low-manipulation.
   - *By necessity* — alopecia, chemotherapy, postpartum shedding, medical hair loss.
   
   This second group needs **discretion, privacy, and dignity** above all. They will decide whether to call you based on whether your site makes them feel like a patient or a client. Recommend a private consultation option and a discreetly worded line — *"private consultations available"* — rather than a clinical section.

**4. The bride and the event client (24–40).** Weddings, graduations, milestone birthdays, photoshoots. Books 3–6 months ahead, brings a party, and posts everything. Highest per-visit spend. Needs trial appointments and firm timelines.

### Secondary audience

**5. Mothers booking for daughters (ages 5–17).** Extremely protective about tension and scalp health — often *because* of a bad past experience. Wins trust through your gentleness, not your gallery. Frequently becomes a client herself.

**6. Relocated and traveling clients.** New to Loudoun County, or visiting, and searching for a braider they can trust sight-unseen. **This person converts almost entirely on your website and reviews** — she has no word-of-mouth to rely on. She is the reason the site has to be excellent.

**7. Natural-hair transitioners (20–35).** Coming off relaxers, unsure what to ask for. Needs education more than a menu. The artistry/technique content converts her.

### Shared psychographics

- Have been burned before — by tension, by shedding, by a five-hour appointment that ran to nine.
- Research hard: Instagram, TikTok, Google reviews, and asking friends before booking a stranger.
- Judge a salon by its gallery first and its professionalism second.
- Value time. A salon that respects the clock reads as premium.
- Care about scalp and edge health, increasingly, and are wary of anyone who doesn't mention it.

### What they are actually buying

Not braids. **Confidence and reclaimed time.** Six weeks of not thinking about their hair, and knowing it looks right the whole time.

### Copy implications

- Lead with outcome and reassurance, support with technique.
- State appointment lengths honestly. "6–8 hours" builds more trust than silence.
- Say the word *tension* out loud. Your audience is listening for it.
- Never use "unruly," "nappy," "manageable," "tame," or "fix" about textured hair. Use *texture*, *pattern*, *density*, *length retention*, *protective*.
- Show real client work at real angles, including the parts and the scalp — that's what a knowledgeable client actually inspects.

## 5. Brand Personality

**Archetype:** The Artisan meets The Caregiver — a master of a craft who also takes care of you while doing it.

### Five traits

| Trait | What it means | How it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** | Inviting, never clinical or cold | Warm color temperature, soft light, generous spacing, welcoming copy |
| **Reverent** | Treats hair, heritage, and the craft with respect | Braiding described as a tradition and a discipline, never a "service package" |
| **Expert** | Deep technical knowledge, shared freely | Explains parting, tension, and hair prep — teaching, not gatekeeping |
| **Unhurried** | Nothing is rushed, everything is deliberate | Slow motion, long-form gallery, no countdowns, generous appointment windows |
| **Elevated** | Premium in every detail | Editorial typography, considered photography, no clip art, no neon |

### Voice

Warm, confident, knowledgeable. Speaks like a master braider who genuinely enjoys explaining the work. Uses "we" and "your." Comfortable with technical terms and always defines them. Never talks down, never oversells.

### Tone by context

- **Hero:** evocative, short, emotional.
- **Services:** clear, specific, honest about time and price.
- **Education / artistry:** generous and detailed — this is where you win trust.
- **Booking & policies:** direct, friendly, unambiguous. Deposits and lateness stated plainly, without apology.
- **Medical / hair loss:** quiet, dignified, private. No pity language, no "suffering from."

### Voice examples

> ✅ "We part in sections, not in a hurry. A clean part is what makes a style sit right for six weeks instead of two."
> ❌ "We do the BEST braids in Ashburn!! 🔥🔥 Book now!!"

> ✅ "Knotless braids start with your own hair and feed the extension in gradually. There's no knot at the root, so there's no pull at the root."
> ❌ "Our amazing knotless braids are super gentle and totally pain-free!"

> ✅ "Most full-head styles run 5 to 8 hours. We'll tell you which when you book, and we'll start on time."
> ❌ "Appointment times may vary."

> ✅ "Hair loss has a lot of causes and none of them are your fault. Consultations are private, and we'll talk through options with no pressure."
> ❌ "Suffering from hair loss? We can fix it!"

### Never

Emoji in website copy (fine on social, never on the site). ALL-CAPS shouting. Exclamation marks in body copy. Fake urgency or countdown timers. Naming or comparing to other Ashburn salons. Before/after framing that implies the "before" was shameful. Stock photos of hair that isn't your work.

## 7. Typography Direction

All fonts from Google Fonts — free, self-hostable, variable weights available.

| Role | Font | Weights | Why |

### Typographic rules

- Headlines are tight and large. Body is open and generous — `1.7`+ leading throughout, because this site does real teaching and people will actually read it.
- Body copy max-width: **68ch.** Never full-bleed paragraphs.
- Eyebrow labels are numbered and gilt: `01 — THE HAIR`, `02 — THE HAND`, `03 — THE HOLD`.
- Service prices use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` so columns align.
- Cormorant italic appears **at most twice per page.** It is seasoning.
- No text-shadow. No letter-spacing on body copy. No all-caps paragraphs.
- Fraunces at `display-xl` should always sit on a dark background with real breathing room — at least 1.5× its cap height in clear space above and below.

## 8. Logo Direction

### The mark — "The Bead"

Status: **selected and shipping.** Supersedes "The Halo", the A-monogram drawn for the former name — that mark was built on the *A* of "Angelic" and does not survive the rename.

**Concept: two braid strands descending into a single gilt bead, reading as an M.**

- The **strands** are two clean strokes: each rises from the baseline, turns at the top, and runs diagonally inward. No tapering, no flourish.
- They stop just short of meeting. The **bead** — a filled Gilt circle — sits at the junction and closes the letterform.
- Read fast, it is an **M**. Read slowly, it is two lengths of braided hair finished with a bead, which is the literal craft.
- The bead is the only Gilt element, and it is the same device as the dot in `Mirabelle.B`. Mark and wordmark carry one idea.

**Construction rules** — these are the shipping values, not a proposal. Source of truth: the inline SVG in `website/index.html` and `website/public/favicon.svg`.

- 100 × 100 grid, `viewBox="0 0 100 100"`.
- Left strand: `M18 84 V26 L46 58`. Right strand: `M82 84 V26 L54 58`.
- Bead: circle centred (50, 62), `r` 8. **Filled, never stroked** — at 16px a ringed dot fills in anyway, and the bead is the one part that must survive that size.
- Strands: `fill: none`, stroke 6u, round caps and joins, colour `currentColor` so the mark inherits its context.
- **Favicon variant** carries one extra unit of stroke (7u) and a slightly larger bead (`r` 8.5) so it holds at 16px: strands `M22 82 V28 L47 58` and `M78 82 V28 L53 58`, on a Deep Noir rounded square (`rx` 16).
- Minimum clear space = strand stroke weight × 4 on all sides.
- Verified legible at 16 × 16 px, at 28px in the site header, and at 22px in the footer.

### The wordmark

`Mirabelle.B` set in **Fraunces 600**, with one customization:

- The **dot is set in Gilt `#D2A24C`** at full size on the baseline — never a superscript, never a separator. Optical tightening of about `-0.02em` either side keeps the three parts reading as one word at display size.
- Everything else in the wordmark is Ivory (or Deep Noir when reversed). The dot carries the gilt on its own, so the wordmark needs no divider and no second colour.

In the hero the wordmark splits at the dot — `Mirabelle.` travels left, `M` travels right — which is why the dot belongs to the left half in markup.

### Lockups

1. **Horizontal** (default) — mark left, wordmark right. Site header, footer, email signature.
2. **Stacked** — mark centered above, `Mirabelle.B` below. Signage, packaging, social avatar, business cards.
3. **Mark only** — favicon, app icon, mirror decal, loading state, watermark on gallery images.
4. **Mark + slogan** — stacked lockup with *Crowned by hand.* in Cormorant Garamond italic beneath. Reserved for the hero and print.

### Color applications

- **Primary:** Ivory `#F6EFE7` strands and wordmark, Gilt `#D2A24C` bead and dot. On Deep Noir or Espresso. This is the default.
- **All-gilt:** entire lockup in Gilt. For signage, foil print, mirror decals, and the packaging seal.
- **Reversed:** Deep Noir on Ivory, bead and dot stay Gilt. Invoices, receipts, light documents.
- **Mono:** single-color Ivory or Deep Noir throughout, bead included. Embroidery, stamps, watermarks.

### Motion signature (loader and hero reveal)

The two strands draw upward from their base points over 500 ms (`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`), then the bead scales in from 0 over 250 ms starting at 450 ms, settling with a soft Gilt bloom. Total ≤ 1 s. Plays once — never loops.

### Physical applications

Gold foil on matte black business cards (stacked lockup). Brass mirror decals at each station (mark only, 60 mm). Embroidered on Ivory towels and stylist aprons (mono). Gold foil seal on aftercare kit boxes (stacked). Etched or painted gold on the storefront glass (horizontal).

### Do not

Add scissors, a comb, a hair strand illustration, a crown graphic, or a woman's silhouette. Never place the mark inside a circle or badge container *except* the favicon, where a Deep Noir rounded square is the intended treatment. Never outline it. Never rotate it. Never use a drop shadow. **Never let the strands actually touch** — the gap the bead fills is the mark. Never stroke the bead instead of filling it.

## 9. Services Description

### The foundation — hair sourced and processed in-house

This is the business's core differentiator and it deserves its own section on the website, ahead of the service menu.

Most salons order hair from a distributor and find out what they got when they open the bundle. Mirabelle.B sources and processes its own — which means the hair is inspected, cleaned, sorted by texture and length, and quality-checked **before** it ever reaches a client's head.

**What that gives the client:**
- **Traceability.** We know where the hair came from.
- **Consistency.** The bundle you get in March matches the one you got in January.
- **No surprise shedding or matting.** Processing is controlled, not outsourced.
- **Texture matching.** Sorted in-house, so we can match your natural pattern instead of approximating it.
- **No silicone coating.** Hair isn't chemically coated to feel good in the packet and tangle by week two.

> *We grow it, we process it, we braid it.*

**Website treatment:** this section needs real photography of the actual process — sorting, washing, wefting, inspection. Not stock. It is the single hardest claim for a competitor to copy, and unphotographed it's just a sentence.

### Braiding

The core craft. All styles built on clean parting and tension-conscious installation.

| Style | Notes | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Knotless braids** | Feed-in method, no knot at the root, no root tension. The premium default. | 5–8 hrs |
| **Box braids** | Traditional knot base. Classic, durable, versatile. | 5–8 hrs |
| **Cornrows** | Straight-back, freestyle, or design. Scalp braids of any complexity. | 2–4 hrs |
| **Stitch braids** | Precision-parted, clean-lined feed-ins. | 3–5 hrs |

## 11. Visual Mood

Warm, dark, and intimate — **candlelight, brass, and skin.** The reference is a dimly lit atelier at golden hour, not a bright white salon under fluorescents.

### Lighting

- **Warm and low-key.** Color temperature 2700–3200K throughout. Every light source in frame reads golden.
- **Key:** a large soft source, close and slightly above, wrapping around the subject. Soft shadow edges — this is a warm brand, so no hard-edged light.
- **Practicals in frame:** warm bulbs, brass sconces, a lit mirror, a candle. Visible light sources make a space read as *inviting* rather than *lit for photography*.
- **Rim:** a soft warm-gold rim to separate the subject from the dark background. Never cold or blue.
- **Fill:** meaningful, unlike a hard product shoot. Roughly 3:1 key-to-fill — moody but never murky. **The client must always look beautiful, not obscured.**
- **Background** falls away to Deep Noir `#100B09` at the frame edges.

### Skin tone — the single most important rendering note

This brand serves Black women, and most AI generation and default camera grading handles deep skin tones badly. Every image and video must be judged against these:

- **Expose for the skin, not the background.** Let the background go dark; keep the skin correctly exposed.
- **Protect the highlights on the skin.** Skin should have a soft sheen, never a blown-out specular hotspot.
- **No orange or yellow cast.** Warm lighting, neutral skin. Warmth comes from the environment and the gold accents — not from a filter dumped over the person.
- **Never crush shadow detail on the face.** Facial features must stay readable in the dark areas. This is the most common failure and it is disqualifying.
- **Show a range of complexions** across the gallery, from light to deep.
- **Show a range of textures** — 3C through 4C — and don't over-represent looser patterns.

Any image that fails these gets rejected, no matter how good it looks otherwise.

### Materials — what the camera must sell

- **Hair:** the individual strand, the sheen along the length, the crispness of a fresh part, the geometry of a cornrow row.
- **Hands:** mid-motion, fingers working, sectioning. Hands are the hero of this brand — *Crowned by hand* has to be visible, not just stated.
- **Brass and gold:** warm glints on mirror frames, fixtures, hardware. Small, precise, never blown out.
- **Wood and velvet:** walnut stations, oxblood upholstery, texture in shadow.
- **Skin:** soft, healthy sheen. Rendered with care.

### Environment

- Deep warm-dark interior, walnut and brass, oxblood velvet seating, large softly lit mirrors.
- Backgrounds always defocused — a mirror's glow, a warm sconce, hanging bundles of hair softly out of focus.
- Three subjects maximum in any frame. Uncluttered stations.
- No bright white salon walls. No fluorescent tubes. No retail product shelving in frame.

### Camera style

- **Slow, patient, deliberate.** Slow push-ins, gentle arcs, macro detail work. Never fast.
- **Shallow depth of field** — f/1.8 to f/2.8 equivalent. One detail sharp, everything else falling away.
- **Macro emphasis:** fingers crossing three strands, a comb drawing a clean part, a loc being palm-rolled, a needle passing through a weft.
- **Portrait framing:** three-quarter and profile favored over straight-on. Let the style be the subject.
- **Film character:** subtle warm halation on highlights, gentle grain, slight lens softness at the edges.
- **Forbidden:** handheld shake, whip pans, snap zooms, drone shots, time-lapse, fast cuts, spinning chairs.
- Shoot 48–60 fps, present at 24 fps for weighted slow motion.

### Motion & effects

- **Web motion:** everything eases with `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` over 700–1000 ms. Elements rise 24 px and fade in on scroll. Nothing bounces, nothing spins, nothing slides in from off-screen.
- **Gilt shimmer:** a slow, subtle travelling highlight along gold rules and the logo on reveal. Once, never looping.
- **Dust and light:** fine warm motes drifting through the key light beam. Barely perceptible — atmosphere, not an effect.
- **No parallax on portraits.** It distorts faces and looks cheap.

### Color grade

- Warm, rich, and deep. Lifted blacks with a red-brown bias — **never pure crushed black**, which would make the site feel cold and contradict the whole direction.
- Golds and ambers fully saturated. Greens and blues pulled down hard — there should be almost no cool color anywhere in the imagery.
- Skin kept true and neutral within the warm environment.
- Fine grain at 2–3%. Subtle vignette, no more than 12%.

### Overall atmosphere

> A warm room, late afternoon light going gold, hands working slowly and expertly. Unhurried. Cared for. Nowhere else to be.

### Anti-patterns — never produce

Bright white or grey backgrounds. Clinical salon lighting. Cool or blue tones anywhere. Neon signage. Stock photos of women who obviously aren't clients. Before/after grids with an unflattering "before." Cluttered product shelves. Hair floating on a plain background. Crushed, underexposed faces. Anything that looks like a beauty-supply store ad.

## 12. Website Goal

### Primary goal

**Convert visitors into booked, deposit-paid appointments.**

Success metric: completed bookings. Secondary: consultation requests, and phone calls from mobile.

### The four jobs of the page, in order

**1. Introduce.** In five seconds a visitor must understand: this is a premium Ashburn braiding salon that specializes in braids, locs, wigs, and extensions, and it operates at a higher standard than what she's used to. Name, hero image of real work, specialty, and location, all above the fold.

**2. Create desire.** Warm cinematic imagery and a gallery of real work must make her want *this specific salon* before she reads a single price. Desire is built visually; it's confirmed by the technique explanation.

**3. Educate.** This is the differentiator and the client's explicit priority. Explain the in-house hair sourcing, the tension-conscious method, the parting, the aftercare. **Every technique explained is an objection removed.** This is also what makes the site rank — informational content about knotless braids and loc maintenance is what people search for.

**4. Drive to book.** Persistent, low-friction path to a booked appointment. Transparent pricing, honest durations, clear deposit policy, and an obvious phone number for people who'd rather call.

### Conversion architecture

- **Primary CTA — "Book Your Appointment"** — in the hero, after the services section, after the gallery, and in the closing section. Sticky header CTA appears once the hero scrolls out.
- **Secondary CTA — "Request a Consultation"** — for wig, loc-journey, and bridal clients who need a conversation first.
- **Tertiary — a tap-to-call phone number** in the sticky header on mobile. A meaningful share of this audience will call rather than fill out a form. Don't make them hunt.
- Every CTA is Gilt `#D2A24C` with Deep Noir text. Nothing else on the page is that color.

### Trust requirements — this audience will not book without them

Real photographs of real work (never stock) · transparent pricing or honest ranges · realistic appointment durations · a clearly stated deposit and cancellation policy · Google reviews embedded or quoted with attribution · stylist names and specialties · exact address, map, parking guidance, and hours · prep instructions before the appointment · aftercare guidance after.

### Local SEO requirements

This is a location business — the site lives or dies on local search.

- Google Business Profile fully completed, categorized, and linked.
- `LocalBusiness` + `HairSalon` schema markup with address, hours, geo, price range, and services.
- Location language in the H1, title tag, and meta description: *Ashburn*, plus *Loudoun County* and the specific neighborhood.
- Individual pages (or deep-linked sections) per major service — "knotless braids Ashburn," "loc retwist Ashburn," "custom wigs Ashburn" — these are the actual searches. Worth also covering the neighbouring searches: Sterling, Leesburg, Brambleton, and "braids near Dulles."
- NAP (name, address, phone) identical everywhere it appears, on-site and off.
- Embedded map, plus parking and transit guidance.

### Non-goals

No newsletter modal on arrival. No countdown timers. No fake scarcity ("3 slots left!"). No chatbot popup. No auto-playing audio. No pop-up asking for a review before she's even been a client.

### Performance & technical targets

LCP < 2.0 s · CLS < 0.05 · Lighthouse ≥ 95 · **mobile-first — assume 70%+ of traffic is a phone** · gallery images lazy-loaded, WebP, responsive `srcset` · hero video ≤ 4 MB, poster-first, `preload="none"`, muted, looping, with a static image on mobile and under `prefers-reduced-motion` · tap targets ≥ 44 px · fully keyboard-navigable with visible Gilt focus rings · all motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
